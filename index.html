Remote release for the Mamiya RZ67, without the cable.

A tiny Bluetooth trigger that plugs into the RZ67's electrical remote port, and a phone app to fire it, for Android and iPhone. Instant shutter, bulb exposures and self-timer countdowns, all from your phone. Rev 2 is in regular use and shooting film.

The parts

Two pieces, one camera.

Each piece lives in its own repository with its own build instructions, releases and issue tracker. This page is the map.

openrz67-trigger

rev 2 in regular use

ESP32-C3 firmware and a custom PCB. Two isolated PhotoMOS channels close the camera's S1/S2 contacts. Runs off a 250 mAh LiPo at an 80 MHz clock and 0 dBm BLE for long standby.

  • Instant release, bulb mode, configurable countdown
  • KiCad project, Gerbers, BOM and pick-and-place files
  • 3D-printable enclosure, print-tested and in regular use

How it works

Two switches to ground. That's the whole interface.

The RZ67's remote port exposes two contacts, S1 and S2. Shorting them to camera ground fires the shutter. The trigger does exactly that with optically isolated PhotoMOS relays, so the ESP32 never shares a ground with the camera.

BLE commands

The trigger advertises as OpenRZ67. Commands are single bytes written without response. Value = button × 10 + state.

ByteAction
11Fire shutter, 100 ms pulse
21 / 20Start / end bulb
31 / 30Start / cancel 10 s countdown
3 n 1Countdown of n seconds
Every board exposes the standard Device Information Service (firmware revision). Rev 3 boards add a read/notify characteristic with the system voltage in millivolts, plus the standard Battery Service (0x180F) with the level in percent.
For bulb exposures set the shutter dial to B. The camera closes the shutter on its own after about 60 seconds regardless.
The app

Three modes, no menus.

Direct release, countdown, bulb. Switch modes at the top, fire with the big button. Follows your system light or dark theme.

Get started

Build one.

A hobby project, so there is nothing to buy: you build one yourself, with the board made by JLCPCB. Read the trigger README before ordering.

  1. Get a board

    Order the verified rev 2 from its fab package: Gerbers, BOM and pick-and-place exactly as ordered, KiCad source at tag pcb-rev2. Rev 3 is in pcb/kicad/out, still a work in progress.

  2. Flash the firmware

    Clone the trigger repo and run pio run -t upload with PlatformIO. That flashes rev 2; for rev 3 use pio run -e rev3 -t upload. Hold BOOT while resetting if upload doesn't start.

  3. Make the cable

    Four-pin JST XH at the board end; at the camera end, four 2.54 mm Dupont sleeves pushed onto the pins and taped. Wire all four pins straight through; pin 1 (6 V) ends on a pad the board leaves unconnected. Double-check orientation before you plug it in. The cell, switch, antenna and connectors are listed in BOM.md.

  4. Print the case

    Get the enclosure files and printing instructions from case/ in the trigger repo. Fits rev 2 and rev 3, with the battery under the PCB. Read the case README before printing.

  5. Install the app

    Grab the APK from releases. Power on the trigger and the app connects on its own. On iPhone, build openrz67-ios in Xcode until it is in the App Store.

Roadmap

Where it's heading.

  • done
    Rev 2 in regular use

    PhotoMOS relays instead of mechanical, side-entry camera connector, BQ25185 charger and TPS63031 buck-boost. Tested on USB and battery, September 2026.

  • in progress
    Rev 3

    Safer battery handling, battery level reporting and general board refinements.

  • in progress
    Coin-cell board

    An nRF54L15 module on a CR2032, with no charger or regulator. Rev A is ordered, not yet tested. Design, firmware and case in nrf52/.

  • done
    Print-tested enclosure

    Stacked snap-fit case with the cell under the PCB. In regular use, with minor refinements ongoing.

  • in progress
    App Store release

    The iOS app works against the trigger. Next: an Apple developer account and review.

  • ideas
    More of the RZ67 system

    The remote port is only the first thing on this camera worth opening up.
