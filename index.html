A Bluetooth remote release for the Mamiya RZ67.

A 37 mm box on a CR2032 that plugs into the RZ67's electrical remote port. An Android or iPhone app fires the shutter over Bluetooth, holds it open for bulb, or runs a countdown. The board sleeps between uses and wakes on a button press.

The project started in 2023 on a Seeed XIAO ESP32-C3 and a Fritzing schematic. A custom ESP32-C3 board on a LiPo followed in 2025 and went through two fabricated revisions. The coin-cell board is the third design; rev A is its first order, after several layout rounds.

openrz67-trigger

rev A in testing

An nRF54L15 module on a CR2032, with no charger, regulator or power switch. Two isolated PhotoMOS channels close the camera's S1/S2 contacts. Zephyr firmware, updates over Bluetooth.

  • Instant release, bulb mode, configurable countdown
  • Comes assembled from JLCPCB, nothing to solder
  • 3D-printable case with a sliding floor for the cell

How it works

The RZ67's remote port exposes two contacts, S1 and S2. Shorting them to camera ground fires the shutter. The trigger does that through two PhotoMOS relays, so the cell and the camera share no ground.

Camera remote port

Four pins, viewed from the front of the camera.

PinSignal
16 V, not connected
2GND
3S1 switch
4S2 switch

Button and power

No power switch. The chip sleeps until the button wakes it.

StateWhat happens
AsleepAbout 0.7 µA by spec. A press wakes it.
AwakeAdvertises as OpenRZ67 for 5 minutes, then sleeps if no phone connects.
ConnectedStays on while the app holds the link. After a disconnect, 5 more minutes to reconnect.
Hold 3 sOff, until the next press.

BLE commands

Commands are single bytes written without response. Value = button × 10 + state.

ByteAction
11Fire shutter, 100 ms pulse
21 / 20Start / end bulb
31 / 30Start / cancel 10 s countdown
3 n 1Countdown of n seconds
The board also exposes the standard Device Information Service (firmware revision), a read/notify characteristic with the cell voltage in millivolts, and the standard Battery Service (0x180F) with the level in percent.
For bulb exposures set the shutter dial to B. The camera closes the shutter on its own after about 60 seconds regardless.

The app

Mode selector at the top, one large release button. Light or dark theme follows the system setting.

Building one

For now you order the board assembled from JLCPCB yourself; the rest plugs in. A kit with a finished board, and perhaps a printed case, may come once the design has settled. Rev A has not been signed off yet, so read the nrf README before ordering.

  1. Get a board

    Upload the Gerber zip, BOM and position file from nrf/kicad/out to JLCPCB, with assembly on both sides.

  2. Flash the firmware

    Build it with the nRF Connect SDK, then flash once over the SWD connector with a Raspberry Pi Pico 2 running Raspberry Pi's debugprobe firmware, cell out. After that, updates go over Bluetooth. Steps in nrf/firmware.

  3. Make the cable

    A Qwiic / STEMMA QT pigtail with female Dupont ends, pushed onto the camera pins and taped: black on pin 2 (GND), blue on pin 3 (S1), yellow on pin 4 (S2). The red wire is not connected on the board. Double-check orientation before you plug it in.

  4. Print the case

    Get the files from nrf/case: shell and sliding floor in ABS, no supports, and a light pipe in clear filament. The CR2032 goes in + side up.

  5. Install the app

    Grab the APK from releases. Press the button and the app connects on its own. On iPhone, build openrz67-ios in Xcode until it is in the App Store.
